Typhoon Surigae Makes Closest Approach to Okinawa as Japan's Storm-Battered Season Grinds On

Typhoon Surigae made its closest pass by Okinawa's main island on Sep 27, packing a central pressure of 990 hPa and sustained winds around 108 kph after strengthening from tropical-storm status over the preceding two days, per News On Japan (Sep 27). Forecasters expect it to deepen further — potentially to 955 hPa with 216 kph winds by Tuesday — before curving northeast on a track passing south of the Izu Islands near Kanto, with low odds of a direct landfall, per The Star (Sep 26). Okinawa faced gusts of 20–25 meters per second and waves over 3 meters, a fresh test for a region still clearing landslides from Typhoon Dujuan's record rainfall days earlier.

02

Atlantic Heads for First Hurricane-Free September Since 1994

The 2026 Atlantic hurricane season is on pace to produce zero hurricanes in September for only the third time since World War II — after 1962 and 1994, both also El Niño years — against a seasonal average of five hurricanes by Sep 28, per weather.com meteorologist Jonathan Belles (Sep 26). A strong El Niño is suppressing storm formation while the Bermuda High and jet-stream flow steer any systems that do form toward Europe rather than the US coast. Forecasters cautioned the shutout isn't assured to hold, noting past El Niño years still produced late-season storms like 2015's Hurricane Kate and Joaquin, the latter causing $2 billion in indirect US damage without making landfall.

Magnitude-7.0 Quake and 6.6 Aftershock Rattle New Caledonia, No Tsunami or Casualties

A magnitude-7.0 earthquake struck 68km east-northeast of Tadine in New Caledonia's Loyalty Islands at 8:23am local time on Sep 25, followed a day later by a magnitude-6.6 aftershock centered 252km northeast of Noumea at a shallow 6km depth, per Anadolu Agency (Sep 26) and TASS (Sep 26). The US National Tsunami Warning Center confirmed “there was no tsunami warning, advisory, watch or threat in effect following the quake,” and no casualties or significant damage were reported from either tremor in the sparsely populated French Pacific territory.
