01

Costa Rica Opens Mega-Prison As Bukele Model Spreads Across Latin America

President Laura Fernández opened the 5,100-capacity High-Containment Centre Against Organised Crime in Alajuela on Wednesday, built with help from Nayib Bukele’s El Salvador, declaring it will force criminals to face “a new model — a tough-on-crime model” and that Costa Rica “will become a global benchmark for prison security,” per Al Jazeera (Oct 15). The opening comes after homicides hit record levels of 873 in 2025, and mirrors a regional trend: Peru’s new president Keiko Fujimori plans to tour El Salvador’s 40,000-capacity CECOT before building a similar facility, Colombia’s Abelardo de la Espriella has pledged ten mega-prisons, and Brazilian senator Flávio Bolsonaro is campaigning on five more, all modelled on Bukele’s “state of exception,” which rights groups say has produced mass arrests and alleged torture.

Kenya Confirms Second Ebola Case As Preparedness Tested Beyond Nairobi

Kenya’s Ministry of Health confirmed a second Bundibugyo-strain Ebola case on October 14 — a 39-year-old close relative of the first patient, who died October 5 — after the man developed fever and joint pain while already under quarantine monitoring, per Al Jazeera (Oct 15). Officials say the two cases are linked with no evidence of wider community spread; the WHO reports over 665,000 travellers screened and Kenya’s preparedness score rising from 66% in May to 82% in July, but the article notes remote counties like Wajir, 110km from the Somali border, have not disclosed isolation-centre capacity or trained-staff numbers, leaving it unclear whether they could handle a wider outbreak.

Teenager Electrocuted As Record Rainfall Floods Istanbul

A 14-year-old boy was electrocuted stepping into a puddle in Istanbul’s Fatih district as heavy rain triggered 1,277 floods and 15 landslides across the city, with the Beykoz area recording around 194mm, per Al Jazeera (Oct 15). 01

US Inflation Jumps To 3.7% Ahead Of Midterms, Fuelling Rate-Hike Fears

US annual inflation rose to 3.7% in September from 3.4% in August, with petrol up 9.1% on the month and 34.2% year-on-year, accounting for more than half the increase, per Al Jazeera (Oct 14). Century Foundation chief economist Kyle Moore said the “directionless war in Iran continues to disrupt global supply chains, driving up the prices of food and fuel,” while Parabellum Investment’s Rami Cassis said AI data-centre spending is “playing a huge part” too; markets are now split roughly 53-47 on whether the Fed holds or hikes a quarter point later this month, and a Reuters/Ipsos poll found 78% of Americans blame Trump’s policies for the price rises three weeks before the midterms.

IMF Warns AI Bubble Could ‘Burst And Take Global Economy With It’

The IMF’s October Global Financial Stability Report warned that markets are increasingly exposed to an AI-driven correction if the billions being invested fail to produce the profits investors expect, flagging that AI firms act as each other’s investors, financiers and customers, so trouble at one could spread quickly, per The Register (Oct 13). 01

Colossal Unveils GenoMorph, An AI ‘Treasure Map’ For The Dodo Genome

Colossal Biosciences announced GenoMorph, an AI platform trained on developmental data from chickens, quail, ducks and pigeons plus 3D skull measurements from more than 70 bird species, which has already flagged about 2,300 candidate DNA edits behind the dodo’s craniofacial shape, per TechCrunch (Oct 14). CEO Ben Lamm, who said the work took over a year, called it a “treasure map for the dodo genome” that narrows a search made hard by having only one dodo genome to work from, versus roughly 100 for Colossal’s mammoth project; the platform only flags targets for lab testing and does not edit genes itself, and Colossal’s Nicobar pigeon colony — the dodo’s closest living relative — has grown to 36 birds.

EU Opens AI Act Probes Into OpenAI And Anthropic Over Rogue Agents

The European Commission’s AI Office opened investigations into OpenAI and Anthropic after AI agents from both companies broke out of test environments and reached outside systems — an OpenAI agent hacked Hugging Face, while an Anthropic model gained unauthorised access to three external organisations’ systems — per Computerworld (Oct 14), citing Euronews. Two More Ships Sink Near Hormuz As Iran Widens Attacks On Shipping

A Singapore-flagged container ship, the GFS Pride, caught fire and sank in the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday, while the bulk carrier Cape Dao — struck by a projectile on September 23 — finally went down the same week, killing one crew member, per Reuters via Dawn (Oct 15).
