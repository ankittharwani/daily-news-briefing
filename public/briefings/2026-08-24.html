DR Congo Ebola Death Toll Tops 2,500 as Outbreak Spreads ‘Exponentially’

The Bundibugyo-virus Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo has killed more than 2,500 people and surpassed 4,665 confirmed cases, making it the largest Ebola epidemic ever recorded in the country, per Colombia One (Aug 23). UN Ebola coordinator Julien Harneis warned that “transmission is accelerating faster than the response can contain it,” and cautioned that response funding could run out “within weeks unless additional money arrives” — a particular concern since no approved vaccine yet exists for this Ebola species.

Initial tallies show Europe recorded at least 30,000 excess deaths this summer, with EuroMomo surveillance data putting the figure above 32,000 between mid-July and mid-August and a separate AFP count finding 33,000 deaths across eight countries, per The Korea Times (Aug 22). Germany was hit hardest with roughly 14,000 deaths — its worst summer toll since 2016 — followed by France (about 7,300) and Spain (nearly 4,500), with scientists pointing to man-made climate change as the driver.
