01

Landslide-Dammed River Floods Nepal Village, Leaving Five Missing

A dry landslide blocked the Budhi Gandaki river on Saturday above Chumnubri village in Nepal’s Gorkha district, and the resulting flood swept away about a dozen houses, two suspension bridges and a highway bridge linking Gorkha with Dhading district, per Al Jazeera (Oct 11). Nepal’s disaster authority said two women and three men remain missing despite rescuing at least 82 people, including nine foreigners, while local official Laxmi Gurung called the flood “very scary” and said “it still feels like a nightmare for us.” The Budhi Gandaki is a tributary of the Trishuli River, where a glacier collapse in August triggered a catastrophic flood that killed 1,457 people with thousands still missing, and authorities have now warned residents downstream in Tanahun, Chitwan and the Nawalparasi districts.

02

Panama Hit by Twin Earthquakes and 45 Aftershocks, Tsunami Warnings Issued

A magnitude-7.7 earthquake struck Panama’s Pacific coast on Friday, followed by a second magnitude-6.6 quake and more than 45 aftershocks recorded by the University of Panama, per Fox Carolina/AP (Oct 9). The US Geological Survey put the epicenter near Pitaloza Arriba in Herrera province; a historic building’s tower and several balconies collapsed in Penonomé, one person was hit by falling debris, cracks appeared in the Penonomé cathedral, and Tocumen International Airport briefly suspended operations. The US National Weather Service issued tsunami warnings for Panama, Colombia, Ecuador, Nicaragua and Guatemala, while President José Raúl Mulino urged calm, writing on X that “what happened should not be used to cause alarm or disorder.”

Operations at Riyadh’s King Khalid International Airport were only “gradually returning to normal” after Thursday’s deadly strike when a second attack hit terminals 3 and 4 on Sunday, per CBS News and The National (Oct 9-11). India Rejects UN Criticism of Nearly 3,000 Detentions at “Cockroach Party” Protest

India's mission to the UN rejected the OHCHR's criticism of Delhi's crackdown on Cockroach Janata Party protesters as “unwarranted interference in the country's internal affairs,” per Indian Defence News (Oct 11). The UN body, through spokesman Thameen Al-Kheetan, had said it was “concerned by reports of mass detentions” of demonstrators, lawyers and journalists and urged “utmost restraint” by police; India countered that it is “the world's largest democracy” with courts available for legal remedies and said the UN should apply the same scrutiny to large protests elsewhere. Delhi Police had detained nearly 3,000 people, including CJP founder Abhijeet Dipke, as they tried to reach Jantar Mantar for a sit-in demanding Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar's resignation; most were later released.

United Kingdom

01

Chancellor Healey Weighs Windfall Levy and Petrol VAT Cut to Close £35bn Budget Gap

Chancellor John Healey faces a roughly £35 billion funding gap ahead of his first Budget on Oct 28, driven by rising health spending, a planned increase in defence spending to 3% of GDP by 2030, and higher borrowing costs that have roughly halved the government's fiscal headroom, per Time News/Bloomberg (Oct 12). Healey is reportedly weighing an oil and gas windfall levy, a tax on excess bank profits and a temporary petrol VAT cut, while keeping Labour's pledge not to raise VAT, National Insurance or income tax; PM Andy Burnham has rejected funding defence shortfalls by cutting welfare. The IFS warned the Treasury against relying on unrealistic spending cuts, saying that would make the Budget “a work of fiction,” while Healey has said his aim is to give households economic “breathing space.”
