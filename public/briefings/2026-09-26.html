Second Storm Bears Down on Japan as Typhoon Dujuan's Death Toll Hits 12

Typhoon Dujuan tore through eastern Japan on Sep 21-22, leaving 12 dead — seven in Chiba, four in Kanagawa, one in Miyazaki — and three missing, after Oshima island logged an all-time station record of 832mm of rain in 48 hours, smashing the previous 2013 mark, per The Watchers (Sep 24). Landslides and embankment failures near Narita destroyed homes and cut power to tens of thousands as rescue crews combed a landslide site in Kamogawa, Chiba. Japan is now bracing for Tropical Storm Surigae, forecast to bring “excessive rainfall and violent winds” that “may cause flash flooding and landslides” across the Ryukyu Islands, including Okinawa, on Sep 26-28, prompting Canada to issue a travel warning, per 604 Now (Sep 24).

US Measles Cases Hit Record 3,659 as CDC Confirms Year's First Death

The US measles outbreak has grown to 3,659 confirmed cases as of Sep 24 — already topping 2025’s full-year total of 2,289 and making 2026 the worst year for the disease since it was declared eliminated in 2000, per the CDC (updated Sep 25). The agency also logged its first nationally confirmed measles death of the year, a figure it cautions “is subject to change” as reviews continue, even as Pennsylvania alone has already recorded 835 cases, 164 hospitalizations and four deaths across 39 counties, per Contagion Live (Sep 25). Cases now span 47 jurisdictions, with 95% tied to active outbreaks and only 10 states meeting the 95% vaccination coverage needed for herd immunity.

04

CRISPR Trial Offers New Way to Fight Blood Cancer Without Harming Healthy Cells

Researchers at Washington University School of Medicine used CRISPR to delete the CD33 protein from donor stem cells before transplanting them into 30 adults with acute myeloid leukemia or myelodysplastic syndrome, achieving 100% engraftment by day 28, according to a trial published in Nature Medicine and reported by ScienceDaily (Sep 25). “We are encouraged by the results ... showing that a CD33-deleted stem cell transplant looks very similar to the outcomes of standard stem cell transplantation,” said Dr. John F. DiPersio, professor of medicine at the university’s Siteman Cancer Center. Seven of the 30 patients died during the study — four from cancer progression, three from transplant complications — but the edited grafts could eventually let doctors target CD33-bearing cancer cells without destroying the donor graft itself.
