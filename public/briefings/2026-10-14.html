01

Former DRC President Kabila Launches Coalition With M23 Rebels In Goma

Former Democratic Republic of Congo president Joseph Kabila launched the Front for the Refoundation of the Nation at a news conference in Goma — the city M23 has held since January 2025 — naming himself head of the coalition with rebel leaders, including AFC/M23’s Corneille Nangaa, as his deputies, per Al Jazeera (Oct 13). Coalition adviser Claude Ibalanky said the group aims to unite resistance forces “to put an end to tyranny by any means necessary, including the use of force,” while Al Jazeera’s Alain Uaykani, reporting from Goma, said “people are making comments across the country that this is another level of the conflict” and that some government soldiers could defect to the former army chief. A DRC military court sentenced Kabila to death in absentia last year for alleged collaboration with Rwanda and M23, which he denies; researcher Yvon Muya said the announcement raises the odds of confrontation with President Félix Tshisekedi, whose second term ends in 2028, and analysts warned it could undermine DRC-M23 peace talks planned for Qatar in November.

Hurricane Simon Weakens Off Mexico As Storm Deaths Reach Five

Mexican authorities raised the death toll from Hurricane Simon and the remnants of former-hurricane Rachel to five — a 71-year-old killed by a collapsing wall in Chilpancingo, Guerrero, and four people in flooding in Maneadero, Ensenada — as Simon weakened to 150 km/h winds about 160 km west of Cabo Corrientes, per The Watchers (Oct 13). The US National Hurricane Center rated its confidence in the storm’s track and intensity “very low” given weak steering currents, with forecast rainfall of 125–255mm raising flood and mudslide risk across Sinaloa and the west-central coast; Mexico has deployed over 22,500 personnel, 1,470 vehicles and 18 aircraft in response, with preventive evacuations and shelters already in place.

01

FBI Arrests OpenAI Engineer Accused Of Bugging Executive Conference Room

OpenAI engineer Abdulrahman Alfozan, 35, was arrested and charged with using a device to intercept oral communications after the company found a recording device — hidden in toilet paper and plugged into a TV’s USB port — in an executive conference room on September 30, per the San Francisco Standard (Oct 13). Prosecutors allege a chatbot account tied to his spouse asked, days earlier, how to build and hide a continuous audio recorder and encrypt its SD card, and that on October 5 he was seen looking behind the TV before leaving a bag containing his ID and a second, similar device in another room. US Attorney Craig Missakian called it “a matter of national security,” and OpenAI, which fired Alfozan the day of his arrest, said it has no evidence he accessed model weights, source code or customer data but believes he intended to plant further devices in sensitive spaces; a detention hearing is set for October 19.

African Union Facilitators Press Rwanda, DRC Rebels On Peace Commitments
