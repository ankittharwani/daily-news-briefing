Typhoon Dujuan Batters Tokyo, Kills Two, Forces 1.6 Million to Evacuate

Typhoon Dujuan slammed into Japan’s Kanto region with winds up to 144 km/h (89 mph), killing at least two people and triggering evacuation advisories for more than 1.6 million residents across Tokyo, Chiba and Kanagawa prefectures, per Al Jazeera (Sep 22). Flooding swamped the coastal city of Kamakura, where Mayor Takashi Matsuo warned “there is flooding in various parts of the city, so travelling by car is dangerous,” while Japan Airlines and ANA scrapped a combined 275 flights and bullet trains between Tokyo and Osaka were suspended. The Japan Meteorological Agency’s Takuya Hosomi cautioned there is “a risk the rain will continue” as the storm tracks north toward the Aleutian Islands.

Far-Right AfD Notches Third State Win as Germany’s CDU Suffers Historic Collapse

Germany’s far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) won roughly 38% of the vote in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania’s state election — its third state victory this year — while Chancellor Friedrich Merz’s Christian Democrats (CDU) crashed from 13% to just 4.9%, the party’s worst result since World War II, losing every seat in the state parliament, per Axios (Sep 21). In Berlin’s simultaneous election, the Left party more than doubled its support to 25.7%, finishing first for the first time ever. AfD leader Alice Weidel declared her party had displaced the CDU as Germany’s “people’s party” and said the CDU “will have to think very carefully” about its firewall against cooperating with the far right, as Merz conceded “reforms have to come.”

Bangladesh’s Measles Death Toll Passes 1,060 as Outbreak Rages On

Bangladesh’s measles death toll climbed to 1,063 after six more children died in the 24 hours ending Sep 21, with health authorities logging 1,221 new suspected cases and a cumulative 181,035 suspected cases nationwide since mid-March, per The Business Standard (Sep 21). Confirmed lab-verified infections have reached 20,646, with nearly 160,000 suspected patients hospitalized and infants under six months — too young to be vaccinated — among the hardest hit. Epidemiologist Prof Mahmudur Rahman said “from the beginning of the outbreak, we have not adequately implemented public health measures,” as the government’s catch-up vaccination drive struggles to keep pace.
