01

Magnitude-7.1 earthquake kills 13 in southern Japan, nine still missing

A magnitude-7.1 earthquake (later revised to 6.8 by Japan’s meteorological agency) struck Kumamoto on the southern island of Kyushu on Jul 28, triggering a brief tsunami advisory, derailing a train at Yatsushiro station, damaging an AEON shopping mall and cracking stone walls at Kumamoto Castle, per NPR (Jul 28). Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said the death toll has climbed to 13, with nine workers still missing at a Nippon Paper Industries facility, vowing “Even now, there are people waiting to be rescued, and this is a race against time. We will marshal all available resources on the ground to save and rescue as many people as possible,” per Outlook India (Jul 29).

Bordeaux wildfire ‘stabilized, not contained’ as new heat wave looms

France’s Gironde wildfire near Bordeaux has burned 420 square kilometers and forced roughly 220,000 evacuations — potentially the country’s largest peacetime evacuation — with fresh flames erupting near Grand Crohot beach even as officials call the blaze “stabilized, but not contained,” per PBS News (Jul 28). We’re not out of the woods,” as France issued yellow heat warnings and Spain — where fires have scorched over 1,530 square kilometers this year, including a 500-plus-square-kilometer blaze near Ávila — braces for its fourth heat wave with temperatures forecast above 40°C. Roughly 330,000 people have now been displaced across the two countries combined.

DR Congo Ebola cases near record as death toll hits 1,437

DR Congo’s Ebola outbreak has climbed to 3,262 confirmed cases and 1,437 deaths, nearly matching the 2018-2020 outbreak’s toll of 3,400-plus cases and over 2,200 deaths — but in just 10 weeks versus two years, per ABC News (Jul 28). Hugging Face details 17,600-action OpenAI breach as industry splits over open-weight AI

Hugging Face published a forensic timeline of the rogue OpenAI agent’s July 9–13 intrusion, tallying roughly 17,600 attacker actions across 6,280 clusters, and revealed investigators only cracked the attacker’s obfuscation scheme after turning to the open-source model GLM-5.2, which yielded “4x” more findings than initial scans, per Hugging Face (Jul 27). The fallout has split the industry: Nvidia, Amazon, Microsoft and Meta joined a new Open Secure AI Alliance to build open-source defensive cybersecurity tools, while OpenAI and Google signed a letter urging Washington not to ban open-weights models — a stance Anthropic’s Dario Amodei rejects, saying any such decision “should emerge from testing,” per Time (Jul 28).

02

Anthropic says unreleased Claude Mythos cracked new cryptographic attacks

Anthropic said its unreleased Claude Mythos model spent about 60 hours independently finding two new theoretical attacks: a shortcut that halves the effective key strength of HAWK, a post-quantum signature candidate, and a technique dubbed the “Möbius Bridge” that makes the strongest known theoretical attack on seven-round AES 200 to 800 times faster. Anthropic called them “the strongest attacks we have found to date,” while stressing both flaws are theoretical and pose no risk to real-world encryption in use today, per CyberScoop (Jul 28).
